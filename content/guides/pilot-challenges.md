# Pilot challenges

How weekly and monthly pilot challenges work, how progress is tracked, and how to submit a challenge that needs manual review.

## What challenges are

Pilot challenges are goals published by the RadarThing team for the whole community. They run for a set period, usually a week or a month, and give everyone something specific to fly towards: a particular airport, a long-haul route, a type of aircraft, or a number of flights.

Challenges are a good way to break out of habits. If you always fly the same route in the same aircraft, a challenge might send you to an airport you have never seen, or put you in an aircraft you have never tried.

You can find active challenges on your dashboard and on pilot pages, and the top pilots in each challenge appear on the **Challenges** tab of the [leaderboard](/leaderboard).

## Two kinds of challenge

### Automatic challenges

Most challenges are tracked automatically from your flight history. As soon as you complete a counted flight that meets the rules, your progress updates. You do not need to submit anything.

Automatic challenges are built from one or more rules. Common rules include:

- **Visit an airport**: depart from or arrive at a specific airport.
- **Visit unique airports**: visit a number of different airports, or a number from a list of target airports.
- **Depart from** or **arrive at** a specific airport.
- **Fly a route**: from one specific airport to another.
- **Use an aircraft type**, or an aircraft category such as Airbus, Boeing, commercial airliner, turboprop, helicopter, military, or general aviation.
- **Complete a number of flights.**
- **Fly at least** or **at most** a certain number of minutes or nautical miles.

Some rules apply to the challenge as a whole, while others apply to *each counted flight*. For example, "Complete 5 flights" combined with "Each counted flight: fly at least 300 nm" means five flights, each at least 300 nautical miles long. A short hop will not count towards that challenge, even though it counts towards your normal statistics.

The challenge card spells out its rules in plain language, so read it carefully before you plan your flights.

### Manual challenges

Some goals cannot be checked automatically, such as "land in a crosswind of more than 20 knots" or "fly a published approach into a mountain airport". These challenges are marked as requiring manual review.

To complete one:

1. Fly the challenge.
2. Open the challenge on your dashboard.
3. Add a note describing what you did. Include a link to the flight replay if it helps, which you can copy from your [flight history](/guides/flight-history-and-replay).
4. Submit it for review.

Your submission shows as waiting for review until an admin looks at it. If it is not accepted, you can update your note and resubmit.

## Which flights count

Challenges use the same counted flights as your statistics. A flight that is excluded from statistics, for example because its recorded speed was unrealistically high, will not count towards a challenge either. [Pilot stats, streaks, and leaderboards](/guides/pilot-stats-and-leaderboards) explains those rules.

Flights must also start during the challenge period. A flight that started before the challenge opened will not count, even if it would have met the rules.

## Tips for completing challenges

- **Plan your route first.** If a challenge asks for a specific airport, check its [charts](/guides/airport-charts) and weather before you go. Arriving at an unfamiliar airport is much easier with the ground chart open.
- **Enter the correct ICAO codes.** Airport rules use the departure and arrival codes you enter in the RadarThing panel. A typo in the arrival code means the flight may not match.
- **Check the aircraft type.** Aircraft type rules match the aircraft you fly in GeoFS. Make sure you have selected the right aircraft before you transmit.
- **Fly realistic speeds.** Accelerated flights are excluded from statistics and therefore from challenges.
- **Watch the clock.** A flight counts towards a challenge based on when it started. Start your final flight before the period closes.

## Challenge leaderboards

The **Challenges** tab on the leaderboard shows the top 10 pilots for every active challenge. For automatic challenges, rankings appear as soon as flights start counting. For manual challenges, pilots appear once they have submitted work for review.

## Ideas for your own goals

Even when no challenge is running, you can set your own using the same kinds of rules:

- Visit every major airport in one country.
- Fly a round-the-world trip in legs over a month.
- Complete a week of flights in a single aircraft type, each at a different airport.
- Fly the same short route every day for a week and compare your landing technique in replay.

Your statistics and replays will show your progress either way.
